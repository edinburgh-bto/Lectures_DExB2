Life cycle of malaria


Metric / Feature	Pre-Erythrocytic (PE) Stage	Blood (Erythrocytic) Stage
Total Parasite Biomass	Extremely Low (≈ 10 to 100,000 parasites)	Extremely High (≈ 10⁸ to 10¹² parasites)
Primary Location	Skin, bloodstream (transit), and liver (hepatocytes)	Red Blood Cells (RBCs) throughout the circulatory system and organs
Growth Dynamic	A single massive burst of replication per infected cell	Cyclical, exponential expansion every 24–72 hours
Clinical Presentation	Asymptomatic (clinically silent)	Symptomatic (fever, anaemia, organ damage)
Immune Impact	Weak natural memory due to low antigenic mass	Hyper-inflammatory response; heavy immune evasion

High risk of death is associated with severe symptoms triggered by blood-stage infection
    - CM, RD, SMA, etc

But naturally acquired immunity to severe disease develops rapidly in children with the number of blood infections
    - Goncalves
    - So most malaria deaths are in the under fives

Malaria vaccines (pre-ery, blood-stage, transmission-blocking)

Only pre-ery have been licensed for widespread use since 2020
    - x children in y countries (3 jabs @ 6,7,9 months + booster 12 months later)

Childhood vaccines give life-long protection

But not current malaria vaccines, they are leaky
    - reduce risk of blood-stage infection but do not eliminate it
    - protection wanes within a few years

If vaccines reduce risk of blood infection they should slow the development of NAI

So once the protective effect of the vaccine wanes the risk of severe disease and death increases so the peak in deaths shifts to older children

Experiment:
    - control group: not given vaccine
    - treatment group: vaccinated
    - record all deaths (diagnosing death by malaria is actually quite difficult as symptoms overlap with other childhood diseases)

How many children need to be in the control and treatment groups: power analysis
